what's black hole?

A black hole is a region of space‑time where gravity is so intense that it creates a one‑way surface called the event horizon. You can cross this surface going inward, but nothing—not even light—can cross outward again. Because no light escapes, it appears black, and because anything that reaches it falls in, it’s called a “hole.” The horizon isn’t a material wall; it’s defined by the fate of any path that reaches it, and locally you wouldn’t notice anything special at the moment of crossing【Episode: Great Papers 02 - How Black Holes Radiate, Hawking 1975, 01:18-02:56】.

The size of the horizon (its area) is set by the black hole’s mass: more mass means a larger horizon, and the horizon’s area is a key property in its physics【Episode: Great Papers 02 - How Black Holes Radiate, Hawking 1975, 02:40-04:15】.


my fav formula is e =mc square

That’s a classic one! In the “Great Papers 01 – Einstein’s Special Relativity” episode the hosts explain that the famous (E = mc^{2}) formula comes from Einstein’s short follow‑up paper of September 1905, where he showed that a body that emits energy loses a corresponding amount of mass – the loss of mass equals the emitted energy divided by (c^{2}) [Source 1, 43:16‑44:55]. They go on to state the result more directly: mass is a measure of a body’s energy content, and the conversion factor between the two is the speed‑of‑light squared, so (E = m c^{2}) [Source 4, 44:35‑46:25].

So the podcast treats (E = mc^{2}) as the bookkeeping consequence of taking the constant speed of light seriously and following its implications for energy and mass.

attention is all u need is a good paper

The 2017 paper “Attention Is All You Need” is celebrated in the podcast as a landmark, high‑impact work.

It showed that the attention mechanism alone could replace the recurrent, sequential machinery that earlier models relied on, letting every word “look at” every other word in a single parallel step — the core claim of the title [Source 4, 06:58‑08:40].
By removing the sequential “running memory,” the model became fully parallelizable, which made it possible to scale the architecture to very large sizes [Source 3, 33:22‑35:14].

So, according to the podcast, the paper is not only good—it’s a pivotal breakthrough that enabled the transformer architecture and the massive language models that power today’s AI.

whats c in my fav formula

“c” is the symbol Einstein (and physicists in general) use for the speed of light in vacuum.

In the velocity‑addition rule the term “c” appears as the ultimate limit that no massive object can exceed — the combined speed always stays below “c” [Episode: Great Papers 01 – Einstein’s Special Relativity, 33:52‑35:26].
When Einstein relates energy and mass, the conversion factor is “c²”, i.e., the speed of light squared — energy = mass × c² [Episode: Great Papers 01 – Einstein’s Special Relativity, 44:35‑46:25].
So in any formula you’re thinking of, “c” stands for the constant speed of light (≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s).

whats black hole

A black hole is a region of space‑time where gravity is so intense that it creates a one‑way surface—the event horizon—that you can cross going inward but can never cross outward. Nothing, not even light (the fastest thing there is), can escape once it passes this boundary, which is why the object appears completely black. The horizon isn’t a physical wall; it’s defined by the fate of any path that enters it. The size of the horizon (its surface area) is set by the black‑hole’s mass【Episode: Great Papers 02 - How Black Holes Radiate, Hawking 1975, 01:18-02:56】. This area is a fixed property of a simple black hole and grows with mass【Episode: Great Papers 02 - How Black Holes Radiate, Hawking 1975, 02:40-04:15】.

whats the crux of best paper published so far

The paper’s core insight is that attention by itself is enough to build a language model—you can throw away the whole recurrent, sequential machinery that everyone assumed was required. By turning every word into a query, key and value and letting each word attend to every other word in a single parallel step, the model becomes fully parallelizable and can be scaled to massive sizes. That scalability is what later gave us the large‑language‑model era of modern AI.

The authors “trust that attention all by itself was enough, and to throw away the sequential machinery that everyone assumed you needed” [Episode: Great Papers 05 – Attention Is All You Need, 34:52‑36:23].
They framed the move as “what if attention isn’t the side‑kick? What if attention is the whole thing? … build a model out of nothing but attention” [Episode: Great Papers 05 – Attention Is All You Need, 06:58‑08:40].
This design lets “each word becomes a query, a key, and a value… every word attends to every other in one parallel step… that fixed long‑range links and crucially made the whole thing parallel, which meant it could scale” [Episode: Great Papers 05 – Attention Is All You Need, 33:22‑35:14].
So the crux of the paper is the radical claim “attention is all you need,” which unlocked parallelism and scaling that now powers most modern AI systems.
